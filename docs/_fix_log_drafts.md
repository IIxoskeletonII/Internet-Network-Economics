## Phase 2B.4.a — Chainalysis adoption index

The 2020 PDF parser silently dropped 10 "Among lowest" countries from page 130 (Cape Verde, Chad, Fiji, Laos, Libya, Mongolia, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, West Bank and Gaza, Zimbabwe), reducing 2020 coverage from 154 to 144. Caught at Checkpoint 1 by manual cross-check of all six years' CSVs against their source PDFs; only 2020 was affected. Repair: rows restored with `score=0`, `rank=NaN`, `rank_note="Among lowest"`, and sub-index ranks transcribed from the PDF. Schema-unified across all six years via a new `rank_note` column. The 2025 file's coverage ceiling at rank 130 was investigated and confirmed to reflect Chainalysis's actual published depth, not a parser truncation — no remediation required. Dependent variable for H2 was set to percentile rank rather than raw `overall_score` because Chainalysis discontinued publishing normalized scores after 2021; raw score is retained as a 2020–2021 robustness appendix. Sub-national/territory drops (10 total): Hong Kong, Macao, Taiwan, Puerto Rico, French Polynesia, New Caledonia, Virgin Islands (U.S.), Aruba, Bermuda, Cayman Islands.

The original `scripts/extract_chainalysis.py` parser was preserved with a prominent warning header documenting all three known issues (the 2020 vision/OCR requirement, the page-130 "Among lowest" omission, and the 2025 coverage ceiling). This is to prevent future re-runs from silently overwriting the manually-corrected CSVs. The canonical source from Checkpoint 2 onward is the CSVs in `data/01_raw/chainalysis/`, not the script output.

## Phase 2B.4.b — World Bank panel 2025 extension via forward-fill

The World Bank indicator pull covers 2020–2024 only; no 2025 values are published yet for the four indicators (GDP per capita, CPI inflation, remittances as % of GDP, Findex financial account ownership). Chainalysis publishes 2025 rankings for 130 countries, which would drop entirely in an inner join. Decision: forward-fill 2024 WB values to 2025 for countries with 2024 data present, no cascade from earlier years. This is defensible given the low year-over-year volatility of the four indicators (the Findex baseline is already treated as time-invariant by design; GDP per capita, remittance share, and inflation move slowly at annual frequency relative to the variation the regression is identifying on). An `is_forward_filled` boolean flag is added so Phase 3 can run a robustness spec excluding 2025 observations. Alternative considered: accepting a 2020–2024 panel. Rejected because it would drop a full post-FTX year (2025) at the cost of ~130 observations, and 2025 is the year where the H2 hypothesis of a strengthening post-crisis relationship would be most observable.

## Phase 2B.4.b — Financial account baseline sourced from 2024 Findex wave

The original Roadmap spec anticipated 2021 as the target Findex year for the `financial_account_baseline` time-invariant control. The World Bank pull contained a 2024 Findex wave (Global Findex 2024) with survey values for 139 of 212 sovereign countries — real new survey data, not carried-forward (e.g., US: 94.95% in 2021, 97.02% in 2024). Decision: take the most recent non-null Findex value per country, yielding 139 countries at 2024, 5 at 2021, 1 at 2022, and 67 NaN. A `baseline_year` column records the source year per country for transparency. The variable remains time-invariant within country (verified by assertion) so it continues to be absorbed by country fixed effects in the two-way FE specification. For pooled and interaction specifications where the baseline enters directly, Phase 3 includes a robustness check interacting `baseline_year == 2024` with the baseline variable to verify the survey-year heterogeneity is not driving results.

## Phase 2B.4.c — Chainalysis–WB inner join and H2 panel construction

The Chainalysis long panel (868 rows, 162 countries, 2020–2025) was inner-joined with the forward-filled WB panel (1,231 rows, 212 countries, 2020–2025) on `(country_iso3, year)`, producing `h2_diffusion_dataset.csv` with 861 rows across 160 countries. Join attrition was 7 rows (0.8%) from 3 sanctioned/conflict countries: Cuba (CUB, 2 rows — WB only has 2020, Chainalysis has 2021/2023), Syria (SYR, 1 row — WB stops at 2022, Chainalysis has 2023), and Yemen (YEM, 4 rows — WB only has 2022, Chainalysis has 2020/2023/2024/2025 with no 2022 overlap). All 3 ISO3 codes exist in both datasets; the gaps are year-level WB data sparsity, not mapping failures. No G20 or top-30 GDP countries were affected. CUB drops entirely from the final panel (0 overlapping years); SYR retains 2 rows (2020–2021); YEM drops entirely (0 overlapping years). A `post_2022` dummy (1 if year ≥ 2023) and the `is_forward_filled` flag from Task A are carried through. Eight countries appear in only 1 year (ATG, LBR, LCA, MCO, MRT, NER, SDN, TCD) and are flagged for exclusion from two-way FE specifications at regression time; they are retained in the dataset for pooled OLS and descriptive use.

## Phase 2B.4.c — Adoption percentile derivation and Among lowest treatment

The H2 dependent variable `adoption_percentile` is derived from Chainalysis's ordinal rank per year. For each year, ranked countries receive `adoption_percentile = 1 - (rank - 1) / (max_rank - 1)`, where `max_rank` is the highest rank value observed in that year's data. This maps rank 1 to 1.0 (highest adoption) and the last-ranked country to 0.0. Countries marked `rank_note == "Among lowest"` (12 rows in 2020 only — restored at CP1) receive `adoption_percentile = 0.0`, placing them below all ranked countries. The denominator uses `max(rank)` rather than `count(ranked rows)` because sub-national territory drops at CP1–CP3 removed some ranked entities (Hong Kong, Taiwan, etc.) while preserving the original Chainalysis rank values for retained countries. Using the row count would produce negative percentiles for countries ranked beyond the file's count (e.g., BRN at rank 155 in 2021 with only 149 rows). The `max(rank)` approach correctly reflects the original ranking universe. Resulting distribution: min = 0.0, median = 0.4948, max = 1.0. The `overall_score` column is retained as non-null for 2020–2021 only (Chainalysis discontinued publishing normalized scores after 2021) for potential robustness use.

## Phase 2B.5 — H4 infrastructure cost dataset repair

The H4 cleaning pipeline (notebook cell 9) contained a critical merge defect: `eth_price_df` was joined to the Etherscan transaction frame on `date` alone, without filtering by `asset`. Because the price file contained both ETH and TRX rows for every date, every Etherscan transaction was duplicated (72,000 → 136,800 rows), with half the resulting `fee_usd` values computed against TRX prices (~$0.10) instead of ETH prices (~$2,000). This silently corrupted all ETH fee statistics in the pre-audit H4 master. Caught at Checkpoint 5 during the price-merge inspection, before the row count anomaly propagated further. Repair: filter `eth_price_df` to `asset == 'eth'` before the merge, with an explicit assertion that no nulls remain post-join. Etherscan intermediate rebuilt at correct 1:1 row count (68,847 after the gas filter, was 136,800). The pre-audit H4 master is preserved in `data/03_processed/ARCHIVE/h4_infrastructure_cost.csv` for comparison.

The Tron fee data was stored in TRX (native units) throughout the pre-audit pipeline and never converted to USD, making cross-rail comparison with Ethereum impossible. Repair: daily TRX price pulled from CoinMetrics (`eth_trx_price_usd.csv`, asset filter `'trx'`) and merged into the Tron intermediate on `date`, with `fee_usd = fee_trx * trx_price_usd`. Post-conversion Tron fee_usd range $0.0006–$9.37, mean $0.45 — consistent with the known economics of TRC20 transfers.

The original ETH gas filter applied a single global 95th-percentile cutoff ($89.34) across the full 2020–2025 window, removing zero rows from low-fee months and disproportionately culling high-fee months. Replaced with a monthly rolling 95th-percentile filter computed independently per month. Per-month thresholds logged to `data/02_intermediate/eth_gas_filter_thresholds.csv` (72 rows, range $0.77 in Jan 2020 to $319.35 in Dec 2021) for full reproducibility. Net removal: 3,153 rows of 72,000 (4.4%), distributed proportionally across months rather than concentrated in the bull-market window.

Tron sample expansion (Roadmap Option A, target n≥1000/month) was attempted via TronGrid's public API (`scripts/scrape_tronscan_expanded.py`). The events endpoint returned Transfer transaction IDs successfully, but the per-transaction fee lookup endpoint (`wallet/gettransactioninfobyid`) hit persistent HTTP 429 rate limits after ~100 calls despite a conservative 12 QPS throttle (vs. the documented 15 QPS limit) and exponential backoff. At the required scale of ~72,000 lookups, this is infeasible without a paid API key. Option B fallback (CoinMetrics aggregate TRX fee metrics) returned HTTP 400 for all fee-related metrics (`FeeTotNtv`, `FeeTotUSD`, `FeeMeanNtv`, `FeeMeanUSD`, `FeeMedNtv`, `FeeMedUSD`) on the Community tier — these metrics are paywalled. Decision: Option C (retain n=20 per month) approved by Claude.ai with mandatory Phase 3 caveats. Within-month coefficient of variation on Tron `fee_usd` has median 1.29 across 72 months and 95% CI half-width averaging 62% of the mean, so monthly Tron means are noisy point estimates. Phase 3 H4 analysis (§3.5) must (1) use medians rather than means as the headline Tron statistic, (2) report 95% CIs explicitly on all Tron-side comparisons, and (3) frame H4 as a "congestion-dependent" cost advantage rather than universal cost superiority — empirically, ETH mean fees undercut Tron mean fees in 6 months of 2025 (post-Dencun L1 fee compression), and this nuance must be presented honestly in Phase 4 narrative rather than buried.

The H4 master dataset (`data/03_processed/h4_infrastructure_cost.csv`, 72 rows × 12 columns) stores raw cost components rather than pre-computed savings, per the Roadmap §2B.5 design constraint. Columns: `month`, `eth_mean_fee_usd`, `eth_median_fee_usd`, `eth_p95_fee_usd`, `eth_tx_count`, `tron_mean_fee_usd`, `tron_median_fee_usd`, `tron_p95_fee_usd`, `tron_tx_count`, `post_nov_2022`, `legacy_pct_fee`, `legacy_flat_fee`. The Phase 5 dashboard parameterizes user-specified transfer sizes at chart time via `legacy_cost(x) = x * legacy_pct_fee + legacy_flat_fee`. Legacy percentage fees are time-varying by year, sourced from World Bank Remittance Prices Worldwide (RPW) global averages of inbound and outbound corridors: 2020 = 5.08%, 2021 = 4.60%, 2022 = 3.95%, 2023 = 3.85%; 2024 and 2025 forward-filled from 2023 (last available WB publication). `legacy_flat_fee` is set globally to 0.0 because WB RPW reports blended percentage averages without a separate flat-fee component — this is an assumption, not a measurement, and the dashboard should treat a corridor-typical $3–5 flat fee as a sensitivity case in Phase 5. ETH transaction counts after filtering range 950–1000 per month (close to the n=1000 cap from earlier scraping); ample for monthly mean estimation.
